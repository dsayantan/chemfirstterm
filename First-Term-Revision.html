S12 marksISC 2025
An aqueous solution is made by dissolving 10 g of glucose (C6H12O6) in 90 g of water at 300 K. If the vapour pressure of pure water at 300 K is 32·8 mm Hg, what would be the vapour pressure of the solution? (C = 12, H = 1, O = 16)
How to see it  · Glucose is non-volatile, so it contributes nothing to the vapour above the solution. Raoult's law then reads p = xsolvent × p°solvent — you need the mole fraction of the water, not of the glucose.
  1. Moles of glucose = 10 ÷ 180 = 0·0556 mol (M = 6×12 + 12×1 + 6×16 = 180).
  2. Moles of water = 90 ÷ 18 = 5·00 mol.
  3. Mole fraction of water xA = 5·00 ÷ (5·00 + 0·0556) = 5·00 ÷ 5·0556 = 0·9890.
p = xA p°A = 0·9890 × 32·8 = 32·44 mm Hg
Common slip  · Using the mole fraction of the solute gives 0·36 mm Hg, which is the lowering of vapour pressure, not the vapour pressure. Relative lowering = xB; vapour pressure = xA p°.

S21 markISC 2026 · Specimen 2025
Why are aquatic species more comfortable in cold water than in warm water?
How to see it  · This is Henry's law, not a colligative property. Henry's law says p = KHx, and KH increases with temperature. A larger KH at the same partial pressure of oxygen means a smaller mole fraction dissolved.
Answer  · Dissolved oxygen falls as temperature rises. Cold water therefore holds more dissolved O2, so aquatic life breathes more easily in it.
The same law explains why a soft drink fizzes when it warms up, and why divers use diluted air — nitrogen's solubility in blood rises with pressure at depth.

S52 marksISC 2019 · Specimen 2024
What is an azeotrope? Why can the components of an azeotropic mixture not be separated by fractional distillation?
Answer  · An azeotrope is a binary mixture that boils at a constant temperature and distils over unchanged in composition, i.e. the vapour has the same composition as the liquid.
How to see it  · Fractional distillation works only because the vapour is richer in the more volatile component than the liquid is. At the azeotropic composition that difference vanishes, so no number of plates can enrich the distillate further.
  • Minimum boiling azeotrope — from a large positive deviation. Ethanol + water, 95% ethanol, boils at 351·1 K. This is why absolute alcohol cannot be made by distillation alone.
  • Maximum boiling azeotrope — from a large negative deviation. Nitric acid + water, 68% HNO3, boils at 393·5 K.

S123 marksISC 2025
(i) What happens if red blood corpuscles are placed in a 5% NaCl solution, which is hypertonic? (ii) Calculate the pressure required to stop osmosis when 40 g of Na2SO4 is added to 1 L of water at 298 K. (Na = 23, O = 16, S = 32, R = 0·0821 L atm K−1 mol−1)

S132 marksISC 2025 · Specimen 2024
Briefly describe the process of reverse osmosis used to desalinate sea water, and state why osmotic pressure is preferred over the other colligative properties for determining the molar mass of a macromolecule.
  • Reverse osmosis. Sea water is put on one side of a semipermeable membrane and a pressure greater than its osmotic pressure is applied to it. Solvent is then driven the ‘wrong’ way — from the solution into the pure water — leaving the salts behind. Cellulose acetate is the usual membrane.

Answer  · Reverse osmosis drives solvent out of the solution by applying more than the osmotic pressure. Osmotic pressure is chosen for macromolecules because it is large enough to measure in dilute solution and needs no heating.

4 · The van't Hoff factor and abnormal molar mass

S202 marksISC 2020 · Specimen 2023
Define colligative properties and name them. Why is the molar mass determined from a colligative property called an abnormal molar mass?
Answer  · Colligative properties depend only on the number of solute particles in solution and not on their nature. The four are: relative lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point, depression of freezing point, and osmotic pressure.
How to see it  · The formulae assume each dissolved formula unit gives one particle. When the solute dissociates or associates that assumption fails, so the calculated mass departs from the true one — higher on association, lower on dissociation. That departure is what ‘abnormal’ names, and the van't Hoff factor i is the correction for it.

K383 marksConcept · Heritage 2024–25
State the two conditions a collision must satisfy to be effective. Define threshold energy and activation energy, and explain their relationship.
How to see it  · Collision theory answers the question “why doesn't every collision react?” The answer has two independent parts — an energy requirement and a geometry requirement — and both must be met simultaneously.
  • Energy condition — the colliding molecules must together possess at least the threshold energy, the minimum energy needed to form the activated complex. Collisions with less energy simply bounce apart.
  • Orientation condition — the molecules must be correctly oriented when they meet, so that the bonds to be broken and formed are properly aligned.
TermMeaning
Threshold energy (ET)The minimum total energy that colliding molecules must possess for reaction to occur.
Activation energy (Ea)The extra energy that reactant molecules must absorb over and above their average energy to reach the threshold.
RelationshipEa = ET − E(average energy of reactants)
Activated complexThe unstable, high-energy arrangement at the top of the barrier in which old bonds are partly broken and new bonds partly formed.
Only a small fraction of molecules — those in the high-energy tail of the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution — clear the barrier, which is why most reactions are far slower than the collision frequency would suggest.

H42 marksISC 2019 · Specimen 2024
Why is chlorobenzene far less reactive than chloroethane towards nucleophilic substitution? Give two reasons.
How to see it  · Two independent effects both make the C–Cl bond in an aryl halide harder to break, and a third makes the approach unfavourable.

  • Hybridisation. The carbon bearing Cl is sp2 in chlorobenzene against sp3 in chloroethane. An sp2 carbon is more electronegative and holds the shared pair more tightly, again strengthening the bond.

  3. In alkoxide ion the charge is localised entirely on one oxygen atom, and the electron-releasing (+I) alkyl group actually intensifies that negative charge, destabilising the ion. Alcohols therefore ionise very little.

5 · Carboxylic acids: acidity and reactions

A172 marksHeritage 2024–25 First Term
The pKa value of 4-nitrobenzoic acid is lower than that of benzoic acid. Explain.
How to see it  · A lower pKa means a stronger acid, so the question is really: why does the nitro group stabilise the carboxylate anion?
Answer  · The –NO2 group is strongly electron withdrawing, by both −I and −R. From the para position it pulls electron density away from the –COO− group, spreading the negative charge and stabilising the conjugate base. A more stable anion means the equilibrium of ionisation lies further right, so the acid is stronger and its pKa lower.
The same reasoning, reversed, explains why 4-methoxybenzoic acid is a weaker acid than benzoic acid: –OCH3 donates by +R and destabilises the anion.

A182 marksISC 2025 · ISC pattern
Arrange in increasing order of acidic strength and justify: CH3COOH, ClCH2COOH, Cl2CHCOOH, Cl3CCOOH.
How to see it  · Every chlorine adds another −I pull on the carboxylate, delocalising the negative charge further and stabilising the anion. The effects are cumulative.
CH3COOH < ClCH2COOH < Cl2CHCOOH < Cl3CCOOH
Answer  · Acidity increases with the number of chlorines: acetic (pKa 4·76) < chloroacetic (2·86) < dichloroacetic (1·29) < trichloroacetic (0·65).
The inductive effect falls off sharply with distance: 3-chlorobutanoic acid is a much weaker acid than 2-chlorobutanoic acid, even though both carry one chlorine.

B32 marksSpecimen 2027
Identify the non-reducing sugar which on hydrolysis gives two reducing monosaccharides. Write the hydrolysis equation and explain why the disaccharide itself is non-reducing.
How to see it  · A sugar is reducing if it has a free hemiacetal (or hemiketal) –OH that can open to a carbonyl. In sucrose both anomeric carbons are tied up in the glycosidic bond, so neither ring can open.
  1. C12H22O11 + H2O  →(H+ or invertase)  C6H12O6 (glucose) + C6H12O6 (fructose)
Answer  · Sucrose. It is non-reducing because its glycosidic linkage joins C1 of glucose to C2 of fructose — the anomeric carbon of both units — so no free aldehyde or ketone group can be released. Its hydrolysis products, glucose and fructose, are both reducing.
The hydrolysis is called inversion: sucrose is dextrorotatory (+66·5°) but the equimolar product mixture is laevorotatory (−20·0°), because fructose's −92·4° outweighs glucose's +52·5°. Hence ‘invert sugar’.

B92 marksSpecimen 2027 · ISC pattern
Which structure of a protein is normally unaffected during denaturation? Explain what happens on denaturation and give two examples.
How to see it  · Denaturation breaks the weak forces that hold a protein folded — hydrogen bonds, disulphide bridges, ionic and hydrophobic interactions — but it does not touch the strong covalent peptide bonds of the backbone.
Answer  · The primary structure is unaffected, since it is nothing but the sequence of amino acids joined by peptide bonds. The secondary and tertiary structures are destroyed, the globule uncoils, and the protein loses its biological activity.
  • Coagulation of egg white on boiling — soluble globular albumin becomes an insoluble fibrous mass.
  • Curdling of milk when it turns sour, the lactic acid formed denaturing the casein.
Denaturation is usually irreversible — a boiled egg cannot be unboiled.

3 · Vitamins, enzymes and nucleic acids

B142 marksISC 2020 · ISC pattern
What are enzymes? State any two characteristics of enzyme catalysis.
Answer  · Enzymes are globular proteins that act as biological catalysts, speeding up the reactions of living organisms under mild conditions of temperature and pH.
  • Highly specific. Each enzyme catalyses one reaction, or one class of reaction — urease hydrolyses urea and nothing else. This is the lock-and-key relationship between the active site and the substrate.
  • Highly efficient, and sensitive to conditions. One molecule can transform millions of substrate molecules per minute, but activity peaks at an optimum temperature (around 310 K) and pH; outside that range the protein denatures and the activity is lost.
Enzymes are named after their substrate with the suffix -ase: maltase, invertase, zymase.
