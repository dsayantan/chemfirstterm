The C–X bond and reactivity

H11 markHeritage 2024–25 First Term
The reactivity order of 1° alkyl halides follows the order:   (a) CH3CH2X < CH3X < CH3CH2CH2X   (b) CH3X < CH3CH2X < CH3CH2CH2X   (c) CH3CH2X < CH3CH2CH2X < CH3X   (d) CH3CH2CH2X < CH3CH2X < CH3X
How to see it  · A primary halide reacts by SN2, in which the nucleophile must reach the carbon from the side opposite the halogen. Every extra carbon on the chain crowds that approach, so reactivity falls as the chain lengthens.
CH3X > CH3CH2X > CH3CH2CH2X    (decreasing)
Answer  · (d) — written as an increasing order, that is CH3CH2CH2X < CH3CH2X < CH3X.
Common slip  · Options (b) and (d) are exact reverses. Decide first whether the list is to be read increasing or decreasing — that is what the question is really testing.

H32 marksISC 2025
An organic compound [A] of formula C4H9Br reacts with aqueous KOH at a rate depending on the concentration of [A] only. A structural isomer [B] of the same formula reacts at a rate depending on the concentrations of both [B] and KOH. Identify [A] and [B].
How to see it  · The rate law names the mechanism, and the mechanism names the structure. First order in the halide alone ⇒ SN1, which needs the most stable carbocation — a tertiary halide. Second order overall ⇒ SN2, which needs an unhindered carbon — a primary halide.
  1. rate = k[A] ⇒ unimolecular ⇒ [A] = (CH3)3C–Br, 2-bromo-2-methylpropane.
  2. rate = k[B][OH−] ⇒ bimolecular ⇒ [B] = CH3CH2CH2CH2–Br, 1-bromobutane.
Answer  · [A] = 2-bromo-2-methylpropane (3°); [B] = 1-bromobutane (1°).
The two mechanisms run in opposite orders: SN1 goes 3° > 2° > 1° on carbocation stability, SN2 goes 1° > 2° > 3° on steric hindrance.

H52 marksISC 2020 · ISC pattern
Arrange in increasing order of reactivity towards SN1 hydrolysis and justify: chlorobenzene, benzyl chloride, 1-chlorobutane, 2-chloro-2-methylpropane.
How to see it  · SN1 rate tracks the stability of the carbocation formed, so rank the cations. A cation that cannot form at all comes last.
  1. Chlorobenzene → a phenyl cation, not resonance stabilised and very unstable — essentially unreactive.
  2. 1-Chlorobutane → a primary carbocation, poor.
  3. 2-Chloro-2-methylpropane → a tertiary cation, stabilised by +I and hyperconjugation.
  4. Benzyl chloride → a benzyl cation, stabilised by resonance with the ring — the most stable of the four.
Answer  · chlorobenzene < 1-chlorobutane < 2-chloro-2-methylpropane < benzyl chloride
Common slip  · Benzyl chloride looks primary and is often placed low. Its cation is delocalised over the ring, so it beats even the tertiary halide.

Ambident nucleophiles

H61 markISC 2026 · Specimen 2026
Fill in the blanks: Methyl chloride on treatment with ______ forms methyl cyanide, whereas on treatment with ______ it forms methyl isocyanide.
How to see it  · CN− is an ambident nucleophile — it can attack through carbon or through nitrogen. Which end attacks is decided by how ionic the reagent is.
  • KCN is largely ionic, so free CN− is present and attacks through carbon (the more nucleophilic end), giving the cyanide (nitrile).
  • AgCN is largely covalent, so only the nitrogen lone pair is available, giving the isocyanide.
Answer  · alc. KCN; alc. AgCN
The same logic governs nitrite: KNO2 gives the alkyl nitrite R–ONO, AgNO2 gives the nitroalkane R–NO2.

H73 marksSpecimen 2027
Two students were given ethyl chloride. Rehan used alcoholic KCN, Ananya used alcoholic AgCN. Rehan's product could be converted to a carboxylic acid on hydrolysis; Ananya's could not give the same acid. Write the equations and the IUPAC names of both products.
  1. Rehan — alc. KCN, ionic, so carbon attacks:
  2. C2H5Cl + KCN → C2H5–CN + KCl  —  propanenitrile (ethyl cyanide)
  3. C2H5CN + 2H2O →H+, Δ C2H5COOH + NH3  —  propanoic acid
  4. Ananya — alc. AgCN, covalent, so nitrogen attacks:
  5. C2H5Cl + AgCN → C2H5–NC + AgCl  —  ethyl isocyanide (ethyl carbylamine)
  6. C2H5NC + 2H2O →H+ C2H5NH2 + HCOOH  —  an amine and formic acid
Answer  · Rehan: propanenitrile, which hydrolyses to propanoic acid. Ananya: ethyl isocyanide, which hydrolyses to ethylamine and formic acid — not the same acid, which is exactly what she observed.
Note the carbon count: the nitrile route adds one carbon, which is why KCN is the standard way of stepping a chain up by one.

Substitution against elimination

H102 marksISC 2023 · ISC 2019
Give chemical equations: (i) ethyl chloride treated with aqueous KOH (ii) ethyl chloride treated with alcoholic KOH.
How to see it  · The solvent, not the base, decides which happens. In water the OH− is heavily solvated and acts as a nucleophile; in alcohol it is freer and acts as a base, pulling off a β-hydrogen.
  1. (i) Substitution — gives the alcohol:
  2. C2H5Cl + KOH(aq) → C2H5OH + KCl
  3. (ii) Elimination — gives the alkene:
  4. C2H5Cl + KOH(alc)  →Δ  CH2=CH2 + KCl + H2O
Answer  · (i) Ethanol. (ii) Ethene.
Common slip  · The words aqueous and alcoholic are doing all the work and must be written into the answer. Omitting them loses the mark even if the product is right.

Substitution against elimination

H112 marksISC 2018 · ISC pattern
What happens when 2-bromobutane is heated with alcoholic KOH? Name the rule that predicts the major product and state it.
How to see it  · Alcoholic KOH eliminates. A β-hydrogen can be taken from either side of the C–Br carbon, so two alkenes are possible and a rule is needed to choose between them.
Answer  · The major product is but-2-ene, by Saytzeff's rule: in a dehydrohalogenation the preferred alkene is the more substituted one — the one carrying more alkyl groups on the doubly bonded carbons — because it is stabilised by hyperconjugation. But-1-ene is the minor product.
Aqueous KOH on the same substrate would substitute instead, giving butan-2-ol.

H122 marksISC 2025 Improvement
An organic compound [A], C3H6O, gives a positive iodoform test. On reduction with LiAlH4 it gives [B]; [B] with SOCl2 gives [C]; [C] with alcoholic KOH gives [D], which on ozonolysis yields formaldehyde and acetaldehyde. Identify [A] to [D].
How to see it  · Work from both ends. A positive iodoform test on C3H6O means acetone. At the other end, ozonolysis giving HCHO and CH3CHO fixes [D] as propene, because cleaving CH3CH=CH2 gives exactly those two.
  1. [A] = CH3COCH3, acetone (has the CH3CO– group the iodoform test needs).
  2. LiAlH4 reduces a ketone to a secondary alcohol: [B] = CH3CH(OH)CH3, propan-2-ol.
  3. SOCl2 replaces –OH by –Cl: [C] = CH3CHClCH3, 2-chloropropane.
  4. Alcoholic KOH eliminates: [D] = CH3CH=CH2, propene.
  5. Check: ozonolysis of propene → CH3CHO + HCHO. ✓
Answer  · [A] acetone, [B] propan-2-ol, [C] 2-chloropropane, [D] propene.
SOCl2 is the preferred chlorinating reagent because both by-products, SO2 and HCl, are gases and leave the flask.

Haloarenes

H142 marksISC 2019 · ISC pattern
Why is chlorobenzene far less reactive than chloroethane towards nucleophilic substitution? Give two reasons.
  • Resonance. A lone pair on chlorine is delocalised into the ring, so the C–Cl bond acquires partial double bond character. It is shorter (1·69 Å against 1·77 Å) and stronger, and does not break easily.
  • Hybridisation. The carbon bearing Cl is sp2 in chlorobenzene against sp3 in chloroethane. An sp2 carbon is more electronegative and holds the shared pair more tightly.
  • Repulsion and instability. The π cloud repels an incoming nucleophile, and the phenyl cation that SN1 would require is very unstable.
Answer  · Chiefly the partial double bond character from resonance, plus the sp2 hybridisation of the ring carbon.
This is why the Dow process needs 623 K and 300 atm to make phenol from chlorobenzene — the bond will not break under mild conditions.

H151 markSpecimen 2023
Assertion: Nitration of chlorobenzene leads to the formation of m-nitrochlorobenzene. Reason: The nitro group is meta-directing.
How to see it  · Chlorine is the substituent already on the ring, and it is the existing substituent that directs the incoming group. Chlorine deactivates by −I but directs ortho and para by its +R effect, so nitration gives the o- and p-products, not the meta.
Answer  · (d) Both Assertion and Reason are false. The assertion is wrong because chlorine is o/p-directing; the reason is irrelevant here because –NO2 is the group arriving, not the one directing.
Common slip  · Deactivating and o/p-directing is unique to the halogens. Every other deactivator is meta-directing, which is what makes this a good assertion–reason question.

H183 marksISC 2020 · ISC 2024 · ISC 2025 Improvement
How will you convert: (i) chlorobenzene to biphenyl (ii) benzene to biphenyl (iii) chlorobenzene to aniline?
  1. (i) Fittig reaction — two aryl halides coupled by sodium in dry ether:
  2. 2C6H5Cl + 2Na  →dry ether  C6H5–C6H5 + 2NaCl
  3. (ii) Benzene must first be chlorinated, then coupled:

H193 marksISC 2025 · Specimen 2026
Write equations to illustrate (i) the Finkelstein reaction (ii) the Swarts reaction (iii) the Wurtz reaction.
  1. (i) Finkelstein — halogen exchange to make an alkyl iodide:
  2. R–Cl + NaI  →dry acetone  R–I + NaCl
  3. (ii) Swarts — exchange to make an alkyl fluoride:
  4. CH3–Br + AgF → CH3–F + AgBr  (Hg2F2, CoF2 or SbF3 also serve)
  5. (iii) Wurtz — two alkyl halides coupled by sodium:
  6. 2C2H5Cl + 2Na  →dry ether  C4H10 + 2NaCl
Why dry acetone in Finkelstein: NaI dissolves in it but NaCl and NaBr do not, so the by-product precipitates and drives the equilibrium forward. That detail is worth a mark on its own.
Common slip  · Wurtz is only useful for a symmetrical alkane. Two different halides give a mixture of three products, so it is never the answer for an odd-carbon chain.

Which one can produce the foul-smelling compound methyl isocyanide in the presence of alcoholic KOH?   (a) chloroform and aniline   (b) chloroform and methanol   (c) chloroform and dimethylamine   (d) chloroform and methylamine

Polyhalogen compounds

H292 marksISC pattern · syllabus 6(v)
Give the structure and one use each of (i) DDT (ii) a freon (iii) iodoform, and state one environmental objection to two of them.
  • DDT, p,p′-dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane — the first modern synthetic insecticide, used against malarial mosquitoes. It is non-biodegradable, accumulates in fatty tissue up the food chain, and is now banned or restricted in most countries.
  • Freons, chlorofluorocarbons such as freon-12, CCl2F2 — refrigerants and former aerosol propellants. In the stratosphere UV light frees chlorine radicals which destroy ozone catalytically; they are being replaced under the Montreal Protocol.
  • Iodoform, CHI3 — formerly an antiseptic, and its action is due to the iodine it liberates, not the compound itself. Its objectionable smell has largely displaced it from use.
Chloroform is the fourth of the named polyhalogens: a solvent and a former anaesthetic, no longer used as one because it damages the liver and is oxidised in air to phosgene.

H302 marksISC 2020
How will you convert propene to 1-bromopropane?
How to see it  · Adding HBr to propene normally follows Markovnikov's rule and puts the bromine on the middle carbon, giving 2-bromopropane. To get the bromine on the end carbon the addition must be reversed.
CH3CH=CH2 + HBr  →peroxide (benzoyl peroxide), Δ  CH3CH2CH2Br
Answer  · Add HBr in the presence of an organic peroxide — the peroxide (Kharasch) effect, or anti-Markovnikov addition. The mechanism is free-radical, not ionic.
Common slip  · The peroxide effect works for HBr only. HCl and HI do not show it, because the corresponding radical steps are respectively too endothermic and too exothermic to propagate a chain.

H314 marksHeritage 2016–17 Pre-Board
An organic compound [A], C3H6O, reduces Tollens' reagent and gives [B] with hydroxylamine. [B] on reduction with LiAlH4 gives [C]. [C] on warming with chloroform and potassium hydroxide gives [D], which has an unpleasant smell. [D] on reduction with Na/C2H5OH gives [E], which forms an insoluble compound with benzenesulphonyl chloride and NaOH. Give the sequence and the structures of [A] to [E].
How to see it  · Two clues fix the chain. Reducing Tollens' means [A] is an aldehyde, so C3H6O is propanal. And the unpleasant smell after chloroform and KOH is the carbylamine test, so [C] must be a primary amine.
  1. [A] = CH3CH2CHO, propanal.
  2. With NH2OH: [B] = CH3CH2CH=N–OH, propanal oxime.
  3. LiAlH4 reduces an oxime to the primary amine: [C] = CH3CH2CH2NH2, propan-1-amine.
  4. Carbylamine: [D] = CH3CH2CH2NC, propyl isocyanide — the unpleasant smell. ✓
  5. Na/C2H5OH reduces an isocyanide to a secondary amine: [E] = CH3CH2CH2NH–CH3, N-methylpropan-1-amine.
  6. Check: a secondary amine with benzenesulphonyl chloride gives a sulphonamide that is insoluble in alkali, because it retains no N–H. ✓
Answer  · [A] propanal, [B] its oxime, [C] propan-1-amine, [D] propyl isocyanide, [E] N-methylpropan-1-amine.
Hinsberg's test is the confirmation: a primary amine gives a sulphonamide soluble in NaOH, a secondary one gives an insoluble sulphonamide, and a tertiary one does not react. That last step is what settles [E].

H322 marksISC 2025
An organic compound [A], C7H6O2, treated with SOCl2 yields [B]. [B] on heating with NH3 gives [C]. [C] with Br2/KOH gives [D], which responds to the carbylamine test. Identify [A] to [D].
How to see it  · Work backwards from the last clue: a positive carbylamine test means [D] is a primary amine, and Br2/KOH on an amide is the Hofmann degradation, which loses one carbon.
  1. C7H6O2 with an acid group → [A] = C6H5COOH, benzoic acid.
  2. SOCl2 → [B] = C6H5COCl, benzoyl chloride.
  3. NH3 → [C] = C6H5CONH2, benzamide.
  4. Hofmann degradation, one carbon lost → [D] = C6H5NH2, aniline — which gives the carbylamine test. ✓
Answer  · [A] benzoic acid, [B] benzoyl chloride, [C] benzamide, [D] aniline.

H333 marksSpecimen 2024
Identify [A], [B] and [C]: C6H5COOH →PCl5 [A] →H2, Pd/BaSO4 [B] →KCN(alc), distil [C].
  1. PCl5 converts the acid to the acid chloride: [A] = C6H5COCl, benzoyl chloride.
  2. H2 over poisoned palladium is Rosenmund's reduction, which stops at the aldehyde: [B] = C6H5CHO, benzaldehyde.
  3. Alcoholic KCN on an aromatic aldehyde, distilled, is the benzoin condensation: [C] = C6H5CH(OH)–CO–C6H5, benzoin.
Answer  · [A] benzoyl chloride, [B] benzaldehyde, [C] benzoin.
Cyanide appears here as a catalyst for a carbon–carbon bond, not as a nucleophile substituting a halide. Same ion, quite different role.

H341 markISC 2024
Assertion: Ammonolysis of alkyl halides involves the reaction between alkyl halides and alcoholic ammonia. Reason: Ammonolysis of alkyl halides produces secondary amines only.
How to see it  · Ammonolysis certainly is an alkyl halide with ammonia in alcohol — the assertion is true. But the product is a mixture: the primary amine formed is itself a nucleophile and attacks more halide, giving secondary, then tertiary amine and finally the quaternary ammonium salt. So the reason is false.
  1. RX →NH3 RNH2 →RX R2NH →RX R3N →RX R4N+X−
Answer  · (c) Assertion is true but Reason is false.
A large excess of ammonia favours the primary amine, because the halide is then far more likely to meet ammonia than to meet the amine already formed.

H351 markSpecimen 2022 Semester 1
The reaction sodium alkoxide + alkyl halide → ether + sodium halide is called: (a) Wurtz (b) Kolbe's (c) Perkin's (d) Williamson's synthesis
Answer  · (d) Williamson's synthesis
How to see it  · An alkoxide attacking an alkyl halide is a clean SN2, which is why the halide must be primary. Put the bulk on the alkoxide, never on the halide.
Common slip  · With a tertiary halide the alkoxide acts as a base instead and the product is an alkene, not an ether. That is the standard follow-up question.
